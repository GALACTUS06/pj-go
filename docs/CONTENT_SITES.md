## 1. Shipbuilders Memorial (`shipbuilders-memorial`)

**Blurb**  
Harborfront Park’s landmark sculpture shows shipbuilders carrying a hull frame toward the water — a public honor to the yards that built Port Jefferson’s name.

**Facts**

- The park occupies waterfront land that once held heavy industry, including oil terminal use before civic conversion.
- The sculpture is the emotional centerpiece of the harbor lawn and pier district.
- Shipbuilding east of Brooklyn once made Port Jefferson a serious maritime workplace, not only a postcard harbor.

**Puzzle role:** Piece of the ferry’s harbor context — “why this shore matters.”

## 2. Ferry Terminal (`ferry-terminal`)

**Blurb**  
The Bridgeport & Port Jefferson ferry terminal is the village’s living bridge across Long Island Sound — scheduled boats still tie New York’s north shore to Connecticut.

**Facts**

- Terminal address area: West Broadway at the harbor.
- The route connects Port Jefferson, NY with Bridgeport, CT.
- For many visitors, the ferry *is* Port Jeff: arrival, departure, and skyline in one glance.

**Puzzle role:** Core of the finale image — the crossing itself.

## 3. Bayles Shipyard (`bayles-shipyard`)

**Blurb**  
Bayles Shipyard is listed on the National Register of Historic Places — chandlery, machine shop, and mould loft from an era when hulls, not boutiques, defined this shore.

**Facts**

- Key surviving industrial buildings date to the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
- Wartime and commercial work passed through these yards before later reuse.
- The machine shop / mould loft fabric later supported civic harbor life (Village Center area).

**Puzzle role:** Industrial half of the ferry story — boats were *made* here long before they were boarded here.

## 4. Historical Society Museum (`historical-society`)

**Blurb**  
The Mather House Museum complex is the village’s attic: shipwright tools, clocks, shops, and sail-loft memory kept by the Historical Society of Greater Port Jefferson.

**Facts**

- The Mather House (mid-19th century) was home to a shipbuilding family and later tied to the Mather hospital bequest story.
- Outbuildings interpret trades and everyday village commerce.
- A harbor diorama / maritime collections help visitors see the waterfront before today’s park.

**Puzzle role:** Narrative glue — how to *read* the other eight stops.
